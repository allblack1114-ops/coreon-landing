Safety Governance · executive safety and health management system

Objectives, resources, training and contractors —
run on the same records as field execution.

So that the executive's safety and health management system does not stop at documents, COREON computes results from the same ledger as field Safety Events, turns shortfalls and gaps into corrective actions, and records confirmation by an authorized person. Only functions verified in production with an internal verification organization in October 2026 are shown.

Objectives · Decree Art. 4(1)

Objectives with computed KPIs

Register annual safety and health objectives on 14 KPIs; hazard closure rate, overdue items, training completion and budget execution are calculated from records. A different executive approves, a missed target opens a corrective action automatically, and someone other than the completer must verify it.

Resources · Decree Art. 4(2), (4)–(6)

Staffing adequacy and budget shortfalls

Required headcount, appointments, qualifications and dedicated assignment are recorded per half-year; a shortfall without a hiring action blocks executive confirmation. Spending beyond the plan is blocked, and shortfall requests are approved or rejected (with an alternative) by a different executive. Appointments, filings and headcount decisions remain with the business.

Training · Decree Art. 5(2)3–4 · OSH Act Art. 29

Training → work authorization → action in one event

When a Safety Event requests work authorization, each worker's training history is checked; untrained workers are denied and recorded, and make-up training is planned from the denial. Work stop cannot be lifted and the event cannot close until authorization is granted after training. Beyond the training register, certificates and make-up training, COREON flags action owners with no training record, recent newcomers without training, and recurring hazards that need refresher training, and reports the post-training understanding check rate.

Contractors · Decree Art. 4(9)

Principal–contractor action control

Principal assignment → contractor action and evidence → principal rejection or verification → stop-work request and release are recorded per contractor, and contractors cannot see each other's events. Contractor capability evaluation criteria are on the roadmap.

Emergency · Decree Art. 4(8) · Act Art. 4(1)2

Incident response lifecycle

Occurrence → emergency response and work stop → executive and labor-office reports (receipt numbers) → site control and scene preservation → resume approved by another person → follow-ups → independently verified root cause and prevention → executive closure. Closure is refused while steps are missing, and the closed record is hash-locked. First-aid, medical decisions and statutory reports remain with the business.

Pilot · Claim → Evidence → Verification

8–12 week field pilot measurement

Closure time, time to first action, overdue rate, evidence completion, open high-risk items and reopen rate are computed from product records for the baseline (customer records or the same-length period before start) and the pilot, with hash-chained weekly snapshots confirmed by the customer executive. Customer field results are published only after a pilot, and metric changes are not presented as causal effects.

Management review (ISO 45001 9.3)Objectives, operating KPIs, half-year duty checks, training and contractor status are aggregated in one record. If a target is missed, a decision on objectives, budget, people or improvement is required before submission; when a different executive confirms, the record is locked with SHA-256 and an RFC 3161 trusted timestamp is requested.

COREON supports recording and confirming procedures. It does not determine legal compliance or guarantee exemption from liability.

Questions we are often asked before adoption.

How does COREON help with Korea's Serious Accidents Punishment Act?

COREON does not provide legal advice or any exemption from liability. It records risk assessments, TBM, corrective actions, evidence and approvals as one event, so executives and safety managers can check whether the safety and health management system was actually carried out. See the law mapping for scope by provision.

How are risk assessment results managed?

Risk assessments, field reports and equipment signals are grouped into one Safety Event and linked to an owner, deadline, corrective action, before/after evidence and residual-risk reassessment. An event cannot close without the required evidence and approval.

Can it connect to existing equipment or monitoring systems?

COREON provides a standard REST/Webhook contract; OPC UA, Modbus and MQTT signals connect through an edge gateway. The actual integration scope is confirmed after interface testing in each customer environment.
